    1.0 Progress Since the Last Review

    The Board last reviewed the campaign on 25 August 2026, eight weeks after the accounts went live. This section covers what has happened since then. It takes the August position as the baseline and follows each platform forward to 22 November 2026.

    1.1Where we were in August

    In August the campaign had made the brand known. Across Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and X it had about 48,000 views, it had been recognised by KTDA through reposts, and people were regularly asking when the product would be available. TikTok was the fastest‑growing channel then, with about 21,000 views. Facebook was close behind at about 19,000.

    1.2Where we are now

    Facebook and TikTok alone have now passed 101,000 views, more than double the total for all four platforms in August. The growth has not been even. Facebook has become our leading channel, rising from about 19,000 to 75,801 views. TikTok has grown only modestly, from about 21,000 to 25,200 views. This change of position is the most important development since the last meeting, and most of this report explains it.

    Cumulative Views at Each Checkpoint
    Reading the chart: In the first month both platforms grew at a similar pace. From August, Facebook speeds up sharply while TikTok levels off. Facebook gained about 56,800 views in the last three months, about three times what it gained in all the weeks before August. TikTok gained about 4,200.
    Views Gained in Each Period
    TikTok gained more in the single month of late July to August (14.3K) than in the three months since. Facebook went the other way.
    1.3Why the shift happened: a change of strategy

    The shift was deliberate. A new brand with no existing audience faces a hard climb on every platform. Posting regularly is not enough when there is no audience to post to yet, and the content is simply ignored. In September we changed approach: grow strongly on the platforms where growth is easier and pays off faster, and then use that momentum on the harder ones. Facebook responded best, so it received most of the effort. We kept posting on TikTok, Instagram and X, but we now judge their results by a different standard for this phase.

    The approach has worked on Facebook. Its cost is that TikTok slowed down. Section 4 shows that gap has now become too wide, and Section 9 proposes how to close it.

    2.2What the scorecard tells us

    The results are mixed, and the pattern is clear. Every measure of visibility is on track: total views, Facebook views and the number of large posts. The measures that are behind are the ones about turning that visibility into something lasting: engagement, followers and sales. The brand is being seen. It is not yet being followed, and nobody can buy from it.

    Board summary: In the next phase, success should be measured less by how many people see us and more by how many follow us, talk to us and ask to buy.

    3.0 Facebook Analytics

    Facebook has become the campaign's main channel. From 1 January to 22 November 2026 the page recorded 75,801 views, 5,286 three‑second video views and 20 hours 24 minutes of watch time. This section looks at how those views built up, which content earned them, and who saw them.

    3.1How views built up over time

    Facebook views did not grow steadily. They came in bursts. From July to September daily views peaked at around 1,000–1,300 on our best days. In mid‑October, after the change of strategy, one day reached about 7,000 views. Then on 2 November the chai post took the page to its highest day of the year, about 18,500 views.

    Peak Daily Views by Phase
    Analysis: Each phase's best day is far higher than the one before. A single strong post can now reach more people in a day than the page reached in its first month. The weakness is the quiet days between peaks. We do not yet have a regular audience that comes back every day. Values are read from the Meta daily views chart.
    Views: Followers vs Non‑Followers
    91.2% of views come from people who do not follow the page. Facebook is sending our posts to new audiences, which is good for reach. But very few of those viewers come back as followers.
    3.2What content earns the views
    Views by Content Type
    Photos (56.6%) and multi‑photo albums (29.8%) bring in 86% of all views. Reels bring in only 13.4%, even though Facebook currently gives video extra reach. Video is our biggest untapped opportunity on this platform.
    How People Find Our Content
    The news feed delivers 81.8% of views and Reels 13.5%. Only 2.8% come from people visiting the page directly. Stories (0%) and Groups (0.3%) contribute almost nothing. Both are free channels we have not used yet.

    4.0 TikTok Analytics

    Over the last 365 days TikTok recorded 25,200 video views, 1,200 likes, 186 comments, 44 shares and 342 profile views, and the account now has 298 followers. In August TikTok was our leading platform. It is now second, and it needs to be looked at honestly.

    4.1The best engagement, but growth has stalled

    TikTok has two very different sides. Its audience is the most engaged we have: 5.7% of viewers like, comment or share, about eighteen times the rate on our biggest Facebook post. But its growth has stalled. It gained only about 4,200 views in three months, after gaining 14,300 in the single month before the August report.

    Engagement Rate: TikTok vs Facebook
    TikTok: (1,200 likes + 186 comments + 44 shares) ÷ 25,200 views = 5.67%. Facebook (17.8K album): (42 + 4 + 10) ÷ 17,800 = 0.31%.
    TikTok Metrics: August vs November
    Likes grew 52% while views grew only 20%. The people who do see our videos engage more than before, but fewer new people are seeing them.
    4.2How viewers find us
    Traffic Sources
    The For You feed delivers 89.1% of views (up from 68.8% in August), so TikTok's recommendations are still working for us. But only 0.4% comes from the Following feed. Our 298 followers rarely see new videos, so each video has to find its audience from scratch. We have also used no trending sounds (0%), which are one of the easiest ways to gain reach.
    Top Search Queries Leading to Us
    People reach us by searching for other tea factories: Kisyet in Bomet, and Kisoro and Kyamuhunga in Uganda. There is real interest in tea‑factory content across the region. “nkumbi” is a misspelling of our name, so captions should include spelling variants.
    4.3Why TikTok slowed

    TikTok's analytics show a series of single spikes: about 1.4K and 1.7K views a day in July, 1.6K and 1.4K in September, 1.8K in October and about 2K in November, with almost no views in between. That pattern points to three causes:

    • Less effort: the change of strategy moved most effort to Facebook. This was planned, but the gap has now grown too wide.
    • No regular rhythm: without a recurring series or fixed posting days, viewers have no reason to come back for the next video.
    • We have not used TikTok's own growth tools: trending sounds, replying to comments with videos, and working with creators.
    Assessment: TikTok is not failing. It is underfed. The audience is engaged and TikTok's recommendations still push our content. What is missing is regular, platform‑native posting. Section 9 sets out a plan to restart it in January.

    5.0 Audience Demographics & Geographic Reach

    The most important finding of this deeper analysis is that Facebook and TikTok reach two different audiences. Facebook reaches an older, mostly male, rural audience around the factory in Murang'a. TikTok reaches a younger, urban audience concentrated in Nairobi. Each needs its own content and, after launch, its own way to buy.

    5.1Age & gender
    Age Distribution: Facebook vs TikTok
    Analysis: On Facebook, 67.8% of viewers are 35 or older, and the largest group is 35–44 (28.2%). On TikTok, 76.0% are under 35, and the largest group is 25–34 (43.8%). The two platforms barely overlap: Facebook reaches established households and farmers, and TikTok reaches young professionals and students. (Facebook's 55–64 and 65+ groups are combined to match TikTok's 55+.)
    Gender (TikTok)
    TikTok: 57% male, 43% female, almost unchanged since August (59/41). On Facebook, men are the majority in every age group.
    5.2Regional distribution in Kenya: live map

    Within Kenya the platforms split again. Facebook's audience is centred on Maragwa (53.4%), the area around the factory, followed by Nairobi (22.7%) and Thika (6.8%). Kericho and Nandi (3.4% each) are also tea‑growing areas. TikTok's audience is concentrated in Nairobi (81.8%), with smaller groups in Kisumu (5.8%) and Malindi (5.6%). Compared with August, the Kenyan audience has spread from the centre to the coast and the west: Kisumu, Malindi and Mombasa are new.

    6.0 Instagram & X: Strategic Assessment

    Instagram and X have been reviewed separately because, under the current strategy, they are deliberately in a different phase. We still post on both. They are not abandoned. But it would be misleading to judge them by the same measures as Facebook at this stage.

    6.1Instagram: growing

    Of the two, Instagram is the one where followers are growing. At the August review it had about 5,000 views from just 10 posts, the best views per post of any platform at that time. Instagram suits the content that already works for us: reels we have already made for Facebook and TikTok can be posted there with very little extra effort. It is the natural next platform to build up, using Collab posts with partners and carousels of our best Facebook photos.

    6.2X (Twitter): why our effort there has not paid off yet

    At the August review X had about 3,000 views from 15 posts, and it has grown little since. This is not a quality problem. It comes from what X rewards. X is built around conversation. It promotes accounts that start debates, reply to others and join topics that are already trending. A new brand account with no existing following, posting brand images, gets almost no visibility there. Posting regularly does not change that.

    For a brand that started with no presence anywhere, building on X first would be the hardest route. That is why it comes later in our plan. When we turn to it, the approach will be to join conversations, not to broadcast:

    ApproachExampleHow we will measure it
    Start debates people want to join“Chai: milk first or water first? Settle this.” · “Is chai and mandazi still Kenya's best breakfast?”Replies per post
    Reply to large Kenyan accounts and trending topicsTimely, witty chai replies (avoiding political topics)Profile visits, mentions
    Polls and threads“Rate your office tea 1–10” · a thread following the leaf from farm to cupVotes, quote posts
    Share our proven contentOur best Facebook and TikTok posts, rewritten to suit XEngagement rate
    Note to the Board: Instagram and X are posted to every week but judged more gently in this phase. On X we will measure replies, quote posts and mentions, not views. Up‑to‑date follower figures for both platforms will be presented at the meeting.

    9.0 Proposed Way Forward

    We propose to keep the strategy that has worked and add what is missing. Facebook continues to lead, but with a new aim: turning viewers into followers. TikTok is restarted in January with a regular posting schedule. X and Instagram are brought forward in February, using the momentum from Facebook and TikTok.

    11.0 Conclusion & Board Requests

    Since the August review the campaign has more than doubled its audience. Facebook has become a strong channel, with two posts above 17,500 views in November. The audience has spread across Kenya, from Maragwa to Malindi, and to 11 other countries. And the comments show that the people we reach already value the product.

    This deeper analysis also shows what remains to be done. The campaign has two distinct audiences that need different approaches. Its viewers are not yet becoming followers. TikTok has been underfed. X needs a conversational approach rather than broadcasting. Above all, demand is building with nowhere for it to go.

    11.1Requests to the Board
    • Approve a product launch date and an official “where to buy”.
    • Provide a WhatsApp Business number for orders and questions, live by 15 December.
    • Approve a small monthly budget to boost posts that are already performing well. Amount at the Board's discretion.
    • Provide an approved response on production costs and farmer payments.
    • Approve PR packages and merchandise production.
    • Note the ginger (“Jinja”) flavour request for product development.

    The audience is in place and the goodwill is real. People are asking for the product by name. With these decisions, the next review can report not only how many people saw Ikumbi Tea, but how many bought it.
